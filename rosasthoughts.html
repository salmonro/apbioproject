Rosa's Thoughts and Ideas

Thoughts on Nanobionics +
-Luciferase enzymes, luciferase substrates, and cofactors are delivered to plants by encapsulating these components in silica, polymer, or other nanomaterials
-Nanomaterial based approaches allow scientists to customize bioluminescent plants (color, glow duration, etc.)
-Although a nanomaterial-based approach avoids some of the challenges associated with genetic modification, it requires more maintenance, material delivery, and is less automatic and long-lasting than what can be achieved through genetic engineering
Circadian System Idea +
Can we utilize natural circadian rhythm systems in plants so that plants “glow” in the night and do not glow in the dark? Can luciferase be activated at certain times of the day and inactivated at others? -> light, temperature, and other conditions.
-What makes an evening gene an “evening” gene and what makes a “morning” gene a morning gene?
-“Molecular clock” proteins can activate or inactivate some of the fluorescent proteins like luciferase
-Specialized glands/organs that contain luciferase, oxygen, luciferin, hispidin and necessary components. Possibly in the leaves
Circadian proteins +
-ZEITLUPE (ZTL): Blue-light photoreceptor proteins that regulate the circadian clock and flowering. Synchronizes plant internal biological clocks with outside signals and day-night cycle
-CCA1, LHY, TOC1. CCA1 and LHY are transcription factors for morning genes. TOC1 is a transcription repressor that peaks in the evening (Millar & Kay, 2000)
-The DET1 protein in Arabidopsis may also play a role in regulating plant circadian systems by repressing “evening” genes such as CCA1/LHY. Basically, DET1 binds to transcriptional regulators including COP10 and DDB1 to form a transcriptional co-repressor complex and repress expression of transcription inhibitors (like TOC1) which then subsequently suppresses expression of CCA1. Turns evening genes off in the morning (Dong et al., 2011)

Ions +
Can we use ions to control these fluorescent genes?
-Nutrient ions such as Ca2+, Mg2+, and Fe2+ play regulatory roles in synchronizing metabolic signals with the circadian clock (Sanchez et al., 2023)
-Rhythmic Mg2+ fluctuations control carbon fixation and sugar accumulation in rice chloroplasts (Sanchez et al., 2023)
-Mg2+ is also an essential cofactor for ATP-dependent enzymes involved in fluorescence in firefly enzymes like luciferase. Binds ATP and facilitates the adenylation of luciferin (Wilson et al., 2019)
Temperature & circadian rhythm +
What role does temperature play in entraining the circadian rhythm?
-Several circadian clock genes, including CCA1, LHY, GI, PRR7, PRR9, have been shown to interact with temperature signals
-Interesting connection to green fluorescent protein (GFP): The transcription factor ELF3 acts as a thermosensor. ELF3 fused with GFP forms glowing speckles in response to higher temperatures (Sanchez et al., 2023)
-Some genes like CCA1 and LHY may regulate circadian systems through rhythmic histone modification of various clock genes like SDG2 and JMJ14 (Huang et al., 2019)
Engineering & promoters +
Can we engineer transcription factors for circadian regulation?
-Circadian promoters like CCA1 or TOC1 regulate rhythmic expression (Harmer et al., 2009)
-LUX binding sites enable circadian repression (Greenwood et al., 2019)
-CCA1/LHY act as morning activators, TOC1/PRR as evening repressors -> is it possible to completely get rid of binding sites for evening repressors in bioluminescence genes without affecting other processes?
-How do we ensure certain metabolic cycles run continuously, even during the night, without inflicting metabolic burden? -> specialized ATP storage compartments that release it in the night? (in certain organs)
-Engineering a transcription factor with a molecular binding domain shape that can bind to the luciferase gene. The transcription factor also can bind to other transcription factors of the light-day circadian cycles
