Ongoing Questions about Circadian-Entrained Bioluminescent Plants
+
Lingering question: How do we make the trees light up only at night and control its bioluminescence? MIT engineers (MIT 2017) are planning on inserting nanoparticles with luciferase inhibitors to “turn off” bioluminescence when desired Circadian system idea: - Can we utilize natural circadian rhythm systems in plants so that plants “glow” in the night and do not glow in the dark? Can luciferase be activated at certain times of the day and inactivated at others? -> light, temperature, and other conditions. “Molecular clock” proteins can activate or inactivate some of the fluorescent proteins like luciferase Specialized glands/organs that contain luciferase, oxygen, luciferin, hispidin and necessary components. Possibly in the leaves Circadian proteins ZEITLUPE (ZTL): Blue-light photoreceptor proteins that regulate the circadian clock and flowering. Synchronizes plant internal biological clocks with outside signals and day-night cycle CCA1, LHY, TOC1. CCA1 and LHY are transcription factors for morning genes. TOC1 is a transcription repressor that peaks in the evening (Harmer 2001) The DET1 protein in Arabidopsis may also play a role in regulating plant circadian systems by repressing “evening” genes such as CCA1/LHY... (Lau 2011)

Question: what makes an evening gene an “evening” gene and what makes a “morning” gene a morning gene? Can we use ions to control these fluorescent genes? “Nutrient ions, such as Ca2+, Mg2+, and Fe2+, are also found to act in regulatory relationships between metabolic signals and the circadian clock” (2023) Rhythmic Mg2+ fluctuations control carbon fixation and sugar accumulation in rice chloroplasts (2023) Mg2+ is also an essential cofactor for ATP-dependent enzymes involved in fluorescence in firefly enzymes like luciferase (2020) Temperature and the circadian rhythm: Several circadian clock genes, including CCA1, LHY, GI, PRR7, PRR9, have been shown to interact with temperature signals ELF3 thermosensor + GFP speckles: (2023) Some genes like CCA1 and LHY may regulate circadian systems through histone modification (2019) Engineering transcription factor idea...
